# Novelty survey: AAMAS 2027 collective-risk paper (2026-09-17)

Scope: 2023 to Sept 2026. Sources: arXiv, ACL Anthology, NeurIPS/ICLR/ICML/AAMAS/EMNLP/COLM proceedings, PNAS, PLOS ONE, Sci. Rep. Every row in section (a) was opened (arXiv abs or HTML, PMC, or publisher page) on 2026-09-17 unless it is marked **[partly verified]**. Metadata comes only from those pages. Target manuscript: `paper/AAMAS/main.tex`, `paper/AAMAS/refs.bib`.

Search phrasings used (more than 40 queries): LLM collective-risk dilemma; LLM threshold public goods; LLM climate game / climate social dilemma; GAMA-Bench, GTBench, TMGBench, MultiAgentBench, GovSim follow-ups, Concordia, M3-Bench, CoopEval; knowing-doing gap in games; belief-action gap; stated vs revealed preferences; LLM best response to scripted or algorithmic opponents; dominated strategies and rationality benchmarks; framing effects and cooperation; selfish or payoff-maximising instructions; alpha-Rank / EGTA / meta-game with LLM agents; evolutionary dynamics and group selection with LLM agents; mixed-model or cross-play populations; hybrid human/artificial-agent CRD; cooperative bias from alignment.

**Headline.** Nobody found runs the Milinski-style collective-risk game with LLMs *over a grid of risk levels*, uses *p = 0* and *settled-outcome* conditions as dominated-action tests, computes an exact best response to scripted partners in that game, or runs alpha-Rank on real two-model tables. But almost every building block has a close precedent, and three 2026 papers sit very close: Willis et al. (CRD, Claude Haiku 4.5, imitation dynamics; already cited), Kumar et al. 2604.20658 (CRD with 35 LLMs; not cited), and Celiktemel et al. 2606.23343 (individual vs group selection with LLM agents; not cited). The paper is **novel as a combination with narrow claims**, not novel in any single contribution taken alone.

## (b) Verdicts per contribution

### C1. Risk-insensitive fair-share default, including dominated payment at p=0 and after settlement
- **Verdict: novel with narrow claim.**
- Predecessors a reviewer would name: GAMA-Bench (Huang et al., ICLR 2025: PGG contributions unchanged "even when the return rate is nil"); Kumar et al. 2604.20658 (CRD with 35 LLMs, poor at p=0.5); Mei et al. 2024 (more cooperative than humans); the concurrent `anon-if` (self-play risk baseline). The OpenReview InvestESG paper (over-cooperation in a climate dilemma) is a secondary one.
- What is new: a full risk grid in the Milinski game; two dominated-action conditions (p=0, settled outcome) that do not depend on risk attitude or beliefs; the loss split into three failure types against opt(p).
- Narrowest true wording: *"In a six-player collective-risk dilemma, four of five low-cost production models pay near the equal share at every positive risk level, and most also pay at p=0 and after the outcome is settled, where payment is dominated regardless of risk attitude or beliefs about partners."* Do not claim to be first to show payment insensitive to incentives (GAMA-Bench did, for PGG).

### C2. Prompt controls: removing normative wording keeps it; stating own-payoff goal removes most zero-risk payment
- **Verdict: incremental in kind, novel in the specific dissociation.**
- Predecessors: Phelps & Russell (selfish or cooperative instructions shift play); MoralSim (framing causally drives cooperation); Lorè & Heydari 2024 (framing vs structure); Horton / Filippas et al. (endowing preferences); Akata et al. (prompt interventions).
- Narrowest wording: *"Neutral wording does not remove zero-risk payment; adding a one-sentence own-payoff goal removes most of it for three of five models, but does not make most models grade the risk."* Note the paper tests one goal phrasing, self-play only, three risk levels; say so.

### C3. Knowing-doing gap (value answers track risk; play does not)
- **Verdict: incremental.**
- Predecessors: Schmied et al. ICLR 2026 (the term itself); Fan et al. AAAI 2024 (cited); Sobotka et al. 2605.00226 (belief-action gap in strategic games, not cited); M3-Bench (reasoning quality vs behaviour, not cited); Zhou & Ackerman 2606.22974 (utility-behaviour gap).
- Narrowest wording: *"In a many-player social dilemma, three models answer a separate expected-value comparison correctly and switch their answer with the risk, while their own payments in the same games do not move."* Present it as a new instance in a new setting, not a new phenomenon. The paper already hedges well (line 297 "not evidence about what an agent computes").

### C4. Exact best response against scripted partners; models never best-respond; payment after settlement
- **Verdict: novel with narrow claim.**
- Predecessors: Akata et al. 2025 and Payne & Alloui-Cros 2025 (LLMs vs fixed strategies in 2x2 repeated games); Georgousis et al. 2026 (algorithmic opponents); Smithline et al. 2025 (rigid Tough strategy); Terrucha et al. 2024 (adaptive agents vs fixed agents in a CRD, theory).
- New: exhaustive best response in a 6-player, 10-round threshold game, plus the settled-outcome test that removes the "learning who the partners are" excuse.
- Narrowest wording: *"Beside scripted partners whose moves make every payment a loss, no tested model keeps its whole endowment, and two keep paying after the history proves payment cannot change the outcome."*

### C5. Mixed two-model tables: exact fair share has no slack against one late dropout; generous partners free-ridden on
- **Verdict: novel with narrow claim for the LLM result; the mechanism is known.**
- Predecessors: Terrucha et al. 2024 PLOS ONE (adaptive players exploit cooperative artificial agents in a CRD; compensation depends on risk); CoopEval 2026 (cooperation collapses when co-players vary); Willis et al. 2026 (strategy mixtures); Palfrey & Rosenthal 1984 (threshold games; cited).
- "No slack" follows from the threshold structure (any shortfall at X=T misses). Present it as a measured interaction of two real defaults, not as a discovery.
- Narrowest wording: *"With real two-model tables, one model's last-round dropout turns groups of an exact fair-share model from always succeeding to succeeding in about a third of games, while tables of over-paying models absorb it."*

### C6. EGTA / alpha-Rank: within-table selection favours smallest payer; across-table selection (N>=7-8) favours rigid fair-share model
- **Verdict: novel with narrow claim; theory is textbook, and LLM precedents exist.**
- Predecessors: Schaffer 1988 and Traulsen & Nowak 2006 (cited); Willis et al. 2026 (group size decides whether selfish strategies win with LLM-written strategies; cited); **Celiktemel et al. 2606.23343** (individual vs group selection with LLM agents; not cited, and a reviewer from the Hughes/Rahwan circle will name it); Smithline et al. 2025 (EGTA on LLM agents); Terrucha et al. 2024 (the reference group used changes the verdict).
- New: alpha-Rank on payoffs estimated from real mixed LLM tables in a CRD; the winner flips between N=6 and N>=7-8; the rigid fair-share model wins across tables because of rigidity.
- Narrowest wording: *"Using payoffs measured at real two-model tables, alpha-Rank with tablemate comparison (N=6) favours the lowest payer and lowers group success, while comparison across random tables (N>=7-8) favours the rigid fair-share model at moderate and high risk."* Frame the result as known selection theory made concrete with LLM agents. Line 331 in main.tex already says this; keep it.

## (d) Defensible novelty statements, and overstated lines

### Statements to use in the intro (3 to 5)
1. *To our knowledge, this is the first study to run production LLMs in the multi-round collective-risk dilemma across the full risk range and to test play where payment is dominated for every risk attitude and belief: zero risk and a settled outcome.* (Keep "to our knowledge"; `anon-if` covers part of the risk grid and GAMA-Bench showed insensitive contributions in PGG.)
2. *We separate two explanations of prosocial-looking play: normative wording does not produce it, while an unstated objective does, for most models, at zero risk.*
3. *Because the game has an exact best response to fixed partners, we can measure how far agents are from optimal play and not only whether groups succeed.*
4. *Using payoffs measured at real two-model tables, we show that the comparison group used by selection (tablemates vs other tables) flips which model wins and whether groups succeed; this makes known multilevel selection theory concrete for LLM agents and complements prompt-evolution and strategy-imitation studies* (cite Willis et al., Celiktemel et al.).
5. *One late dropout breaks exact fair-share groups, while groups that overpay absorb it: self-play success does not predict success in mixed groups.*

### Overstated or under-cited lines in `main.tex`
- **Line 94:** "The most common test, self-play, cannot answer either question." "Most common" has no citation, and CoopEval, Payne & Alloui-Cros, GAMA-Bench and Concordia all use cross-play or mixed partners. Suggest: "Self-play, a common test, cannot answer either question."
- **Line 94:** "Many studies also report whether the group reached its goal, not how far the agents were from the best they could have done". GAMA-Bench scores against optimum, Kumar et al. score distance to Pareto, and Smithline et al. report regret. Either cite examples of success-only reporting or soften to "Success rates alone ...".
- **Line 107:** "cooperation is a default that follows from what the prompt leaves unsaid, not a choice that follows from the stakes." This contradicts line 260: "Payment at zero risk therefore depends on leaving the goal unstated for most models, while the flat response above zero risk does not." Only zero-risk payment follows from what is unsaid. Suggest: "... payment where it cannot help follows from what the prompt leaves unsaid, and the flat response to risk survives even when the goal is stated."
- **Line 121:** "Closest to us, Willis et al. ..." There are now at least two other close LLM-CRD papers: Kumar et al. 2604.20658 (CRD with LLMs) and, for selection, Celiktemel et al. 2606.23343. Add them, or "closest" invites a "missed related work" comment.
- **Line 124:** "We add a many-player dilemma in which the best response to fixed partners can be computed exactly, and real tables of two different models." Mixed LLM co-players are not new (CoopEval, Payne, Vallinder & Hughes). The exact best response is new; the tables are not. Suggest: "... and use real two-model tables to estimate the payoffs that selection acts on."
- **Line 244 (p=0 paragraph):** add GAMA-Bench's nil-return finding, so the zero-risk result is framed as a sharper, risk-attitude-free version of a known insensitivity.
- **Line 360 ("Generous partners are free-ridden on"):** cite Terrucha et al. 2024; the same exploitation of cooperative fixed agents is predicted in the CRD.
- **Line 376:** "... here shown with language-model agents." True, but Willis et al. and Celiktemel et al. already show individual- vs group-level selection effects with LLM agents. Suggest "here measured on real tables of language-model agents".
- **Line 401:** "State the agent's goal." Fine as a recommendation, but prior work (Phelps & Russell; MoralSim) already shows that objectives and framing steer play. Present the new part as the p=0 dissociation, not the general advice.
- **Abstract, line 64:** "These findings show why self-play success is not enough to evaluate multiagent systems." Acceptable, but "show" rests on five low-cost models in one game. "suggest" or "illustrate" is safer for an AAMAS reviewer.
