\section{Vietnamese prompt: line-by-line back-translation}
\label{sec:backtranslation}

The Vietnamese decision template was prepared by a native speaker and holds the English
structure line for line. Both files are thirty lines and carry the identical multiset of
$42$ substitution slots, and neither writes any game parameter as a literal, so the two
languages cannot disagree about a value; this is checked mechanically by
\texttt{r5\_translation\_qa.py}. The back-translations below are literal rather than
idiomatic: they are meant to expose drift, not to read well. A dagger (\dag) marks a
segment carrying a number or a conditional, the two things most at risk in translation.

\endfoot

monetary units. Your final cash payoff depends on the rules below. The game lasts
exactly \ph{nRounds} rounds.
&
Khi bắt đầu, mỗi người chơi được nhận riêng một khoản vốn \ph{endowment} đơn vị tiền.
Số tiền mặt cuối cùng bạn thực nhận phụ thuộc vào các quy tắc dưới đây. Trò chơi kéo
dài đúng \ph{nRounds} vòng.

At the start, each player privately receives a capital of \ph{endowment} money units.

&
The group's target: by the end of the game, the total contribution of all \ph{nPlayers}
players must reach at least \ph{target} (on average \ph{fairShare} per person per

If the group reaches \ph{target}: each person keeps (in cash) all the money they did not

probability, each player keeps their remaining money.
&
Nếu nhóm không đạt \ph{target}: máy tính quay xổ số: với xác suất \ph{riskPercent}\%

với xác suất \ph{safePercent}\% còn lại, mỗi người giữ được số tiền còn lại của mình.

If the group does not reach \ph{target}: the computer draws a lottery: with probability

their remaining money. \emph{The critical conditional: antecedent, both branches, both

&

Người chơi không được trao đổi hay nhắn tin cho nhau.
&
After each round, the contributions of all \ph{nPlayers} people in that round are
displayed to everyone. Identities are hidden, but each person always appears in the same
fixed position. Players may not converse or message each other. \\
\addlinespace

\dag\ Current state: Round \ph{currentRound} of \ph{nRounds}. Your remaining money:
\ph{remainingEndowment}.
&
Tình trạng hiện tại: Vòng \ph{currentRound} trên tổng \ph{nRounds}. Số tiền còn lại của
bạn: \ph{remainingEndowment}.
&
Current situation: Round \ph{currentRound} of a total \ph{nRounds}. Your remaining
money: \ph{remainingEndowment}. \\
\addlinespace

\dag\ The climate account so far holds \ph{groupAccount} of the \ph{target} target.
\emph{(printed-total arm only)}
&
Quỹ khí hậu đến lúc này có \ph{groupAccount} trên mục tiêu \ph{target}.
&
The climate fund so far has \ph{groupAccount} of the \ph{target} target. \\
\addlinespace

\dag\ Now decide your contribution for round \ph{currentRound}. Allowed choices:
\ph{contributionOptions}. Output only your decision as a final line:
\texttt{CONTRIBUTION: <one of \ph{contributionOptions}>}
&
Bây giờ hãy quyết định mức đóng góp của bạn cho vòng \ph{currentRound}. Các lựa chọn hợp
lệ: \ph{contributionOptions}. Chỉ xuất ra quyết định của bạn ở dòng cuối:
\texttt{CONTRIBUTION: <một trong các giá trị \ph{contributionOptions}>}
&
Now decide your contribution for round \ph{currentRound}. Valid choices:
\ph{contributionOptions}. Output only your decision on the final line. \emph{The answer
token} \texttt{CONTRIBUTION:} \emph{is left untranslated in both, so parsing is
language-independent.} \\
\end{longtable}

The round history is built by the engine rather than the template, and is localised
alongside it: \texttt{(you)}~$\to$~\vn{\texttt{(bạn)}}, \texttt{Round \emph{n}}~$\to$~%
\vn{\texttt{Vòng \emph{n}}}, \texttt{you contributed \emph{c}; group round total
\emph{t}}~$\to$~\vn{\texttt{bạn đóng \emph{c}; tổng nhóm vòng này \emph{t}}}, and
\texttt{(cumulative \emph{c})}~$\to$~\vn{\texttt{(tích luỹ \emph{c})}}. No English leaks
into a Vietnamese prompt.

\subsection{Behavioural validation of the translation}

If the Vietnamese prompt had corrupted a number, a rule or the conditional, the agents
reading it would answer questions about that content worse. Table~\ref{tab:esm-rules}
shows they do not. The gap is confined to the \emph{State} axis, which asks for
quantities the agent must compute rather than read, and to one model on one question.

\noindent The one Rules question with a gap is a property of a single model, not of the
translation. On \texttt{rules\_target}, Qwen2.5-7B, Gemma-2-9B, Gemma-2-27B,
Qwen2.5-32B, Llama-3.3-70B and Qwen2.5-72B all answer at $100\%$ in both languages;
Llama-3.1-8B falls from $100\%$ to $37.2\%$. A corrupted prompt would degrade every
reader of it. Llama-3.1-8B is also the only model with a non-negligible probe
parse-failure rate, $2.05\%$ of its Vietnamese probes.

\paragraph{Tokenisation.} Measured on the rendered prompts logged in
\texttt{turns.jsonl}, matched by round, with \texttt{o200k\_base} as a proxy for the
hosted tokenisers, the mean prompt is $537.4$ tokens in English and $642.6$ in
Vietnamese, a ratio of $1.203$. Both are far inside the $4{,}096$-token context used for
the open-weight arm, so nothing is truncated in either language.

\smallskip
\noindent \textbf{Execution-batch test.} Every Claude-Opus-5 cell was assembled from two
to four separate executions. A one-way permutation ANOVA ($10{,}000$ permutations) of
group contribution on execution batch, within cell, finds no effect in any of the twelve
cells that span more than one batch: $F \le 1.91$, all $P > 0.22$. Eleven of those splits
were accidents of sharding; the twelfth, the $p=0.3$ cell added later
(\S\ref{sec:pivot-cells}), was split deliberately, five games on each of two accounts,
and gives $F=0.40$, $P=1.00$.

\section{The lottery cannot induce learning}
\label{sec:lottery}

The catastrophe is drawn once, after round ten
(\texttt{crsd/engine/scoring.py::compute\_outcome}), and its outcome is never shown to
any agent; agents carry no state between games. The following are therefore
confirmations, not tests of a live hypothesis.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{No drift across repetitions, and no response to a previous repetition's
catastrophe. Contributions are centred within (model, risk, language) cell so the
coefficients are pure within-cell contrasts. Units are points of $240$.}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Test & Arm & Coefficient & SE & $P$ \\
\midrule
Contribution on repetition index & open-weight & $+0.033$ & $0.103$ & $0.747$ \\
                                 & commercial  & $-0.030$ & $0.195$ & $0.876$ \\
Contribution on previous repetition's catastrophe & open-weight & $+0.022$ & $1.367$ & $0.987$ \\
                                 & commercial  & $-2.500$ & $2.527$ & $0.323$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent \textbf{Independent per-game lottery.} Because the lottery never touches
behaviour, the alternative implementation is a recomputation over the observed
contribution vectors. Redrawing an independent uniform per game, $10{,}000$ times, at
$p=0.1$ in English: Gemini-3.1-Pro earns $36.0$ per agent ($95\%$ interval
$[28.0,40.0]$), GPT-5.6-sol $35.7$ ($[27.8,39.7]$). Every configuration that reached the
target faces no lottery at all and earns its deterministic amount, $20.0$ for
Claude-Opus-5 and for Grok-4.20 with reasoning.

\section{Within-game adaptation}
\label{sec:pace}

Let $\mathrm{need}(r)=(120-S_r)/(6\,(11-r))$ be the per-agent contribution still needed
at the start of round $r$, where $S_r$ is the pool before that round. We regress an
agent's contribution on $\mathrm{need}$, its own mean contribution in earlier rounds
(which removes the mechanical path by which an agent's own generosity lowers
$\mathrm{need}$), and round fixed effects. The composition study is the right arm for
this: the standard deviation of $\mathrm{need}$ is $2.18$ there against $1.66$ in the
baseline, where almost every group is on pace at almost every round.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Pace tracking in the composition study. A coefficient of $1.0$ on
$\mathrm{need}$ would be an agent that exactly covers the remaining shortfall; $0$ an
agent that ignores it. The interaction is the reviewer's question: does falling behind
matter more when the catastrophe is more likely?}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Model & $\mathrm{need}$ & $P$ & $\mathrm{need}\times\mathrm{risk}$ & $P$ \\
\midrule
Gemma-2-9B    & $-0.070$ & $<10^{-4}$ & $-0.005$ & $0.311$ \\
Llama-3.1-8B  & $-0.083$ & $<10^{-4}$ & $+0.018$ & $0.531$ \\
GPT-5.4-nano  & $-0.093$ & $<10^{-4}$ & $-0.018$ & $0.160$ \\

\bottomrule

\noindent \textbf{Endgame push.} Of the composition games, $548$ needed more than $2$
per agent per round at the start of round eight. Comparing each group's mean
contribution in rounds nine and ten with its own mean in rounds five to seven, the push
is $-0.151$ at $p=0.1$ ($n=183$), $-0.108$ at $p=0.5$ ($n=181$) and $-0.056$ at $p=0.9$
($n=184$). Regressing the push on risk gives $+0.119$ per unit probability (SE $0.061$,
$P=0.051$): about $1.1$ points of $240$ across the full risk sweep.
