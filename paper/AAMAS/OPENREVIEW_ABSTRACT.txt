TITLE
When Low-Cost Language Models Cooperate by Default: Incentives, Partners, and Selection in a Collective-Risk Dilemma

ABSTRACT
Language-model agents increasingly act in shared environments where one local choice can expose a whole group to collective loss. We study whether cooperation follows the stakes, the partners, or an unspoken default. In a collective-risk social dilemma, agents contribute to a shared threshold over repeated rounds; a catastrophe destroys savings when the threshold is missed. We evaluate five low-cost production models through self-play, prompt controls, value questions, scripted partners, mixed tables, and payoff-based selection. The main result is a dissociation between cooperative-looking play and incentive response. Four models contribute near the equal share at every positive risk level, and most also pay at zero risk, where contribution cannot help. Removing normative wording leaves this pattern largely intact, whereas stating that only the agent's own cash matters reduces zero-risk payment for most models. Several models answer risk comparisons correctly in separate questions but barely change their actions. Against fixed partners, no model consistently follows the exact best response, and some pay after the outcome is settled. In mixed tables, one late under-contributor can break an otherwise successful group. Under rare-mutation payoff-based imitation, a population of one table favours the smallest payer and harms group success. At moderate and high risk, a larger randomly mixed population instead favours an exact fair-share policy. These selection results are conditional on the base-prompt payoffs. These findings show why self-play success is not enough to evaluate multiagent systems. Evaluations should state the agent's objective, compare behaviour with a computed optimum, test situations where actions are dominated, and measure performance in mixed populations.

KEYWORDS
LLM agents, collective-risk social dilemma, cooperation, best response, empirical game-theoretic analysis, multiagent evaluation
