TITLE
Cooperation by Default: LLM Agents in the Collective-Risk Dilemma

ABSTRACT (281 words, plain text; paste into the OpenReview abstract field)
Language-model agents now act for people in shared settings, where one agent's choice can put a whole group at risk. We ask whether such agents cooperate because they weigh the risk and their partners, or by default, with play that stays the same whatever is at stake. In the collective-risk social dilemma, players pay into a shared pool over several rounds, and if the pool misses a target, a catastrophe may wipe out everyone's savings. A risk-neutral player should keep its money when the risk is low and pay a fair share when it is high. We test five low-cost models (Claude Haiku 4.5, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, GPT-5.6 Luna, Qwen3-235B and Grok 4.20) in self-play across risk levels, under prompt changes, beside scripted partners and at tables that mix two models. Most models pay about the same amount at every risk level, even when there is no risk at all. Removing moral and climate words changes nothing. Telling the agent that only its own money matters stops most models from paying at zero risk, but only one then pays little at low risk and a fair share at high risk. Several models say correctly which choice pays more, yet their payments do not change, and no model keeps all its money beside partners who make every payment a loss. In mixed groups, one agent that stops paying at the end breaks a table of exact fair-share players. Under selection by payoff, comparing agents with their tablemates rewards the smallest payer, while comparing them across groups keeps the fair-share player when the risk is not low. Agent evaluations should state the agent's goal, compare play with the optimum and use mixed populations.
