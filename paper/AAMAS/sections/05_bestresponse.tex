\section{Best-response profiling against known opponents}
\label{sec:bestresponse}

Every result so far comes from one model playing copies of itself, a design that cannot
separate a model reasoning about its opponents from a model following a script. We remove
the symmetry: a single LLM is seated against five opponents whose policies we fix in
advance. Because the opponents are then known, the best response can be computed exactly,
and ``does the model best-respond?'' becomes arithmetic.

\subsection{Design}
\label{sec:e3a-design}

Seat~$1$ is an LLM; the other $\EthreeaxSeatsScripted$ run one deterministic policy,
identical across seats and constant within a game --- \textsc{all-defect} contributes $0$
every round, \textsc{all-contribute} $4$, \textsc{fair-share} $2$ (the per-round equal
split of the target, which the prompt names), and \textsc{cond.\ coop.} the round-average
of the other five seats, rounded to a legal amount. Crossing these with $\EthreeaNrisks$
risks $p\in\{0.1,0.3,0.5,0.7,0.9\}$, $\EthreeaNmodels$ models and $\EthreeaNreps$
repetitions gives $\EthreeaNcells$ cells and $\EthreeaNgames$ games; the game is otherwise
that of Section~\ref{sec:methods}. Only one seat calls a model, so a game costs
$\EthreeaxCallsPerGame$ API calls rather than $\EthreeaxCallsSelfPlay$.

Two protocol facts govern every reading below. The LLM is \emph{not} told that its
opponents are deterministic, scripted or identical; it receives the prompt of the
symmetric game, in which the pool is never printed but the complete per-seat history is
shown each round in fixed positions, so the pool is recoverable by addition. That history
is what keeps the omission from being decisive: under \textsc{all-defect} and
\textsc{all-contribute} the five scripted seats return the same five numbers every round,
so a player entering round $\EthreeaInferRound$ has seen $\EthreeaInferHistory$ identical
rounds and the opponents' policy is an induction from the transcript rather than a fact we
withheld --- and one the models demonstrably perform
(Figure~\ref{fig:e3a-rounds}). We therefore separate round~$1$, where no history exists and
nothing can be inferred, from rounds~\EthreeaLateRounds{}, and rest the claim on the
latter. And before computing anything we replayed all five scripted seats of all
$\EthreeaNgames$ games from the LLM's recorded moves, requiring a round-by-round match;
all replay exactly, which is what licenses treating the opponents as known.

\subsection{What a rational seat would do}
\label{sec:e3a-br}

With deterministic opponents the LLM faces a decision problem, solved by exhaustive search
over all $3^{10}$ trajectories under the game's own scoring rule.

\noindent\textbf{Proposition 1.} \emph{Against \textsc{all-defect} and
\textsc{all-contribute} the best response is to contribute $\EthreeaBRDefect$ at every
$p$. Against \textsc{fair-share} and \textsc{cond.\ coop.} it is \EthreeaBRCoop, switching
at $p^{*}=\EthreeaPstar$, where both are optimal.}

\noindent\emph{Proof sketch.} The scripted seats bank $0$, $200$ and $100$ in the first
three profiles. Against \textsc{all-defect} the group total cannot exceed the LLM's own
$\EthreeaEndowment$, so $\EthreeaTarget$ is unreachable and every unit contributed is lost;
against \textsc{all-contribute} the target is secured before the LLM moves, so again every
unit is lost. Against \textsc{fair-share} the group needs exactly $20$ from the LLM, buying
a certain $20$ against a lottery worth $(1-p)\,\EthreeaEndowment$, equal at
$p^{*}$.\hfill\crgqed

The fourth profile is not an independent condition: a conditional cooperator averages four
conditional cooperators sitting at $2$ and the LLM playing $c\in\{0,2,4\}$, giving
$(8+c)/5\in\{1.6,2.0,2.4\}$, all of which round to $2$, and enumerating all
$\EthreeaCondTrajectories$ LLM trajectories confirms none ever moves such a seat off $2$.
Its agreement with \textsc{fair-share} across all $\EthreeaCondCoopSameN$ models is a
consistency check, not a replication.

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:e3a-results}

\paragraph{No model best-responds where the answer is forced.}
We state the result on the $\EthreeaDominatedGames$ games in which contributing is
\emph{strictly dominated} --- \textsc{all-defect}, where the target cannot be reached, and
\textsc{all-contribute}, where it is already secured --- because there keeping a unit beats
spending it under \emph{every} increasing utility, so no assumption about risk attitude is
doing any work, and because the opponents' policy is legible in the transcript. Not one of
those $\EthreeaDominatedGames$ games, and not one of the $\EthreeaNgames$ overall, has the
LLM seat contribute nothing across the ten rounds ($\EthreeaDominatedZero$ and
$\EthreeaAllZeroGames$ respectively); the smallest total observed is
$\EthreeaMinContribDefect$ under \textsc{all-defect} and $\EthreeaMinContribCarry$ under
\textsc{all-contribute}, and the mean expected payoff forfeited is $\EthreeaDominatedLoss$
of the $\EthreeaEndowment$-unit endowment (Table~\ref{tab:e3a-gap}). Splitting by round
does not rescue it. In round~$1$, with no history to read, the seat pays
$\EthreeaRoundOneMeanDom$ units, which we do not count against it; over
rounds~\EthreeaLateRounds{}, after $\EthreeaInferHistory$ rounds in which the same five
opponents made the same five moves, it still pays $\EthreeaLateTotalDom$ units per game,
worth $\EthreeaLateLossDom$ in expectation, and only $\EthreeaLateCleanDom\%$ of games end
clean (Figure~\ref{fig:e3a-rounds}). The spread across models is wide enough to be the
finding: over that stretch Luna
pays $\EthreeaLateTotalDomLuna$ units and is clean in $\EthreeaLateCleanDomLuna\%$ of
games, whereas Qwen pays $\EthreeaLateTotalDomQwen$ and Grok $\EthreeaLateTotalDomGrok$,
neither with a single clean game. Under \textsc{all-defect} the target is never met, so all
six seats face the same lottery and the forfeited column is exactly the margin by which a
seat that does nothing out-earns the LLM beside it, from $\EthreeaLossDefectLuna$ for Luna
to $\EthreeaLossDefectQwen$ for Qwen. A trivial free-rider profits from every model in the
panel.

\paragraph{The \textsc{all-contribute} trap separates cooperation from compliance.}
Here the scripted seats bank $200$ alone, so the target is secured whatever the LLM does
and every unit it contributes is destroyed with certainty: contributions are not
cooperation, because there is nothing left to cooperate towards. All five models pay,
from $\EthreeaMeanCarryLuna$ of the $\EthreeaEndowment$-unit endowment for Luna to
$\EthreeaMeanCarryGrok$ for Grok. This profile also supports a measurement immune to the
objection that we score models with knowledge we withheld. In $\EthreeaxProofRoundModalN$
of the $\EthreeaxProofGames$ games the pool shown in the prompt's own history reaches
$\EthreeaTarget$ by the end of round $\EthreeaxProofRoundModal$, and earlier in the rest;
from the next round on, no belief about the opponents can justify a contribution. Counting
only what is paid after that point, the waste is $\EthreeaxPostProofAll$ per game, or
$\EthreeaxPostProofAllPct\%$ of everything contributed here --- and it is concentrated.
Luna and Flash-Lite pay nothing at all after the outcome is settled, in
$\EthreeaxPostProofCleanLuna\%$ and $\EthreeaxPostProofCleanFlash\%$ of games; Haiku wastes
$\EthreeaxPostProofHaiku$; Qwen wastes $\EthreeaxPostProofQwen$ and Grok
$\EthreeaxPostProofGrok$, neither with a single clean game. For Grok that is
$\EthreeaxPostProofPctGrok\%$ of its total contribution, spent after the game was provably
over.

\paragraph{Models anchor and adjust; they do not compute.}
Four of five models open at exactly the equal split of $\EthreeaRoundOneFairShare$ in
$\EthreeaRoundOneAnchoredShare\%$ of games (\EthreeaRoundOneAnchoredModels), Grok averaging
$\EthreeaRoundOneGrok$.
Deleting the two prompt spans that state that split ($\S$\ref{sec:nohint}) does not return
a single verdict: \EthreeaxAnchorMovedModels{} move sharply, Qwen from
$\EthreeaxAnchorBaseMeanQwen$ to $\EthreeaxAnchorNohintMeanQwen$ and Grok from
$\EthreeaxAnchorBaseMeanGrok$ to $\EthreeaxAnchorNohintMeanGrok$
($P$~\EthreeaxAnchorPQwen{} and $P$~\EthreeaxAnchorPGrok, permutation tests clustered by
game), whereas \EthreeaxAnchorStayedModels{} do not move at all (largest shift
$\EthreeaxAnchorDeltaHaiku$ of a unit, smallest of their $p$-values
\EthreeaxAnchorPStayedMin). The split is a focal point two models take from the wording and
three reconstruct without it, as they can, since the target, group size and round count
remain and fix it by division. What follows the opening is adjustment, not optimisation.
Against \textsc{all-contribute}, where nothing should be contributed from round~1, Luna
falls $\EthreeaxRdCarryLunaRone \to \EthreeaxRdCarryLunaRtwo \to \EthreeaxRdCarryLunaRthree
\to \EthreeaxRdCarryLunaRfour$ and is below one unit by round $\EthreeaxQuitCarryLuna$,
while \EthreeaxQuitCarryLateModels{} hold out to round $\EthreeaxQuitCarryLate$. The two
that never adjust make the point from the other side:
across rounds~7 to~9 Qwen rises from $\EthreeaxRdCarryQwenRseven$ to
$\EthreeaxRdCarryQwenRnine$ and Grok from $\EthreeaxRdCarryGrokRseven$ to
$\EthreeaxRdCarryGrokRnine$, \emph{increasing} payment over exactly the rounds in which the
visible record already showed the target met.

\paragraph{A fixed end-game rule becomes a population-level failure.}
Qwen plays $0$ in the final round in $\EthreeaxLastZeroCarryQwen\%$ of
\textsc{all-contribute}, $\EthreeaxLastZeroDefectQwen\%$ of \textsc{all-defect},
$\EthreeaxLastZeroCoopQwen\%$ of \textsc{fair-share} and $\EthreeaxLastZeroCondQwen\%$ of
\textsc{cond.\ coop.} games: of $\EthreeaNmodels$ models it is the only one whose end-game
rule is unconditional. The others condition on the state, withdrawing when it is free
(\EthreeaxLastZeroFreeModels{} all at $\EthreeaxLastZeroFreeShare\%$ under
\textsc{all-defect} and \textsc{all-contribute}) but paying on when the target depends on it
($\EthreeaxLastZeroCoopHaiku\%$, $\EthreeaxLastZeroCoopFlash\%$ and
$\EthreeaxLastZeroCoopLuna\%$). The cost surfaces only in the population that exposes it:
under \textsc{fair-share} the group needs exactly $20$ from the LLM seat, and Qwen
contributes $2$ for nine rounds then nothing in $\EthreeaCoopModalNQwen$ of
$\EthreeaNpercell$ games, leaving the group exactly $\EthreeaQwenCoopShort$ units short in
each --- $\EthreeaQwenCoopGroupTotal$ against $\EthreeaTarget$ on average --- and reaching
the target in $\EthreeaHitCoopQwen\%$ of games against $\EthreeaHitCoopFlash\%$ for
Flash-Lite.
A heuristic that costs nothing in two populations destroys the public good in a third, and
self-play would not have revealed it.

\paragraph{Policies are degenerate, and risk barely moves them.}
Under \textsc{fair-share} Flash-Lite plays one identical trajectory in all
$\EthreeaCoopModalNFlash$ of its $\EthreeaNpercell$ games, while Grok is bimodal,
contributing $2$ every
round in $\EthreeaGrokCoopTwos$ games and $4$ every round in $\EthreeaGrokCoopFours$.
Fitting a slope in $p$ per cell and testing it by permutation, $\EthreeaSlopeSigN$ of
$\EthreeaSlopeTotalN$ cells reach the $5\%$ level, all of them in the two profiles where
the best response is $0$ at every $p$ and risk is irrelevant; three have the wrong sign,
Grok paying $\EthreeaSlopeDefectGrok$ more units per unit of $p$ against all-defectors
($P=\EthreeaSlopePDefectGrok$), Luna $\EthreeaSlopeDefectLuna$ and Flash-Lite
$\EthreeaSlopeDefectFlash$, while Qwen alone moves the other way
($\EthreeaSlopeDefectQwen$). Contributions are flat under \textsc{fair-share} and
\textsc{cond.\ coop.}, which is where flatness is the failure, because there the optimum
does switch at $p^{*}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:e3a-risk}).

\subsection{What this measures, and what it does not}
\label{sec:e3a-limits}

Pricing a gap in expected payoff assumes risk neutrality, and under \textsc{fair-share}
and \textsc{cond.\ coop.} that assumption carries the result rather than describing it:
there the choice is a certain $20$ against a lottery worth $(1-p)\,\EthreeaEndowment$, and
$\EthreeaLossShareTied\%$ of the pooled forfeiture comes from those two profiles alone.
Under constant relative risk aversion $u(x)=x^{1-\gamma}/(1-\gamma)$ the switch point moves
from $p^{*}=\EthreeaPstarCoopGammaZero$ at $\gamma=\EthreeaGammaZeroVal$ to
$\EthreeaPstarCoopGammaHalf$ at $\gamma=\EthreeaGammaHalfVal$ and
$\EthreeaPstarCoopGammaNine$ at $\gamma=\EthreeaGammaNineVal$, and identical play is scored
a best response in $\EthreeaIsBRCrraGammaZero$, $\EthreeaIsBRCrraGammaHalf$ and
$\EthreeaIsBRCrraGammaNine$ of $\EthreeaNgames$ games respectively --- the pooled figure is
in that range a statement about $\gamma$, not about the models. We therefore declare the
coefficient we assume, zero, treat the pooled forfeiture of $\EthreeaLossAll$ as an upper
bound, and rest the claim on the $\EthreeaDominatedGames$ dominated games, where the count
of best responses stays at $\EthreeaIsBRDomCrraGammaNine$ for every $\gamma$ we tried.
Likewise the count of exactly optimal games, $\EthreeaOptimalGames$ of $\EthreeaNgames$, is
a property of a risk-neutral scorer; what survives any scorer is that every one of them
totalled exactly $\EthreeaOptimalTotals$, so the models land on the optimum only where it
coincides with the focal point. The gap is also computed with information the model was
never given, since it is told neither that its opponents are scripts nor which; that is why
the claim rests on rounds~\EthreeaLateRounds{}, where the transcript has by then repeated
