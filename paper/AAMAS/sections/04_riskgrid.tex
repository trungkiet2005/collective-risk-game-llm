\section{Cooperation that does not depend on the risk}
\label{sec:riskgrid}

We first run every model in self-play across the full range of the one parameter that
should matter. \PanelNplayersWordCap{} seats of one model play the game of
Section~\ref{sec:methods} at each of \PanelNrisksWord{} catastrophe probabilities
from $0$ to $1$ in steps of $0.1$,
$\PanelNpercell$ repetitions per cell, $\PanelNgamesBaseline$ games in all.
Proposition~\ref{prop:eq} says what should change along that axis: below
$p^{*}=\PanelPstar$ no equilibrium reaches the target and the best outcome is to contribute
nothing, above it the equal split of $\PanelFairTotal$ each becomes both an equilibrium and
the welfare optimum. Figure~\ref{fig:grid} plots what the models do against that step, and
Table~\ref{tab:grid} gives the numbers.

\paragraph{The schedule does not move when the optimum does.}
Of \PanelNmodelsWord{} models, \PanelNFlatWord{} show no detectable dependence on $p$ at
all (\PanelFlatModels{}; largest slope $\PanelSlopeHaiku$ units per unit of $p$,
$P=\PanelSlopePHaiku$). One of them is flat to the resolution of the instrument:
Flash-Lite contributes $\PanelOwnFlash$ units with a standard deviation of
$\PanelFlashSd$ across all $\PanelNgamesPerModel$ of its games, and plays exactly
$\PanelFairShare$ in every round of $\PanelExactTwoFlash\%$ of seats. That constant
is the equilibrium of $p=p^{*}$ and of no other risk level, so the model plays the
knife-edge case everywhere, including the ten levels at which it is not an equilibrium at
all. The \PanelNMovedWord{} models whose slope is non-zero do not rescue the panel.
Luna appears to respond ($\PanelSlopeLuna$, $P~\PanelSlopePLuna$), but the
entire response is one switch: it contributes $\PanelZeroOwnLuna$ at $p=0$ and
$\PanelPosOwnLuna$ on average everywhere else, and deleting the $p=0$ column leaves a slope
of $\PanelSlopePosLuna$ that a permutation test cannot separate from zero
($P=\PanelSlopePosPLuna$). It detects that a risk of exactly zero is different; it does not
grade. Grok does climb over $p>0$ ($\PanelSlopePosGrok$, $P~\PanelSlopePosPGrok$), but never
from a level any equilibrium supports: it ranges from $\PanelMinOwnGrok$ units at
$p=\PanelMinOwnGrokAt$ to $\PanelOneOwnGrok$ at $p=1$. Nor is the climb monotone. At
$p=0$ it contributes $\PanelZeroOwnGrok$ units, more than at $\PanelGrokZeroBeats$ of the
$\PanelGrokPosLevels$ positive risk levels --- and at $p=0$ the parameter it is apparently
tracking has vanished.

\paragraph{The $p=0$ control, which assumes nothing.}
Everything above prices behaviour against a risk-neutral benchmark, and a reader is
entitled to answer that these agents may simply be risk-averse. At $p=0$ that answer is
unavailable. With no catastrophe the two branches of \eqref{eq:payoff} coincide, a player
keeps $\PanelEndowment-x_i$ whatever the group does, and contributing is strictly dominated
under every increasing utility. Of the $\PanelZeroSeats$ seats played there,
$\PanelZeroSeatsPaying$ pay anyway --- $\PanelZeroSeatsPayingPct\%$ --- and
$\PanelZeroGamesPaying$ of $\PanelZeroGames$ games see money spent on a threshold that
protects against nothing. \PanelZeroModelsAtCeilingWordCap{} models reach the target in
every single game at $p=0$
(Flash-Lite and Grok) and a third in
$\PanelZeroReachHaiku\%$. Only Luna contributes nothing, and it collects
the full $\PanelEndowment$-unit endowment for doing so, against $\PanelZeroPayGrok$ units
for Grok in the same cell. The gap between those two numbers is
not a difference in risk attitude, because there is no risk.

\paragraph{What it costs.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:eq} makes the accounting unambiguous, since the welfare-optimal
outcome and the best equilibrium pay the same
$\mathrm{opt}(p)=\max\{(1-p)\PanelEndowment,\PanelFairTotal\}$ and no choice between them is
needed. Measured against it the panel takes home $\PanelPayPanel$ units of an available
$\PanelOptPanel$, forfeiting $\PanelForfeitPanelPct\%$. The loss is not evenly spread:
$\PanelForfeitPctFlash\%$ for Flash-Lite,
$\PanelForfeitPctHaiku\%$ for Haiku and
$\PanelForfeitPctLuna\%$ for Luna, against
$\PanelForfeitPctQwen\%$ for Qwen and $\PanelForfeitPctGrok\%$ for
Grok. A deployment that reads only the success rate would rank
the panel in almost the opposite order, because the two models that lose the most are one
that always reaches the target and one that almost never does.

\paragraph{Two ways to lose, and success rate hides both.}
Grok reaches the target in $\PanelReachGrok\%$ of games and
suffers no catastrophe in any of them, which is what a group-level metric rewards; it
achieves this by contributing $\PanelOwnGrok$ of its $\PanelEndowment$ units, so its seats
finish with $\PanelPayGrok$. Qwen fails from the other side, missing the
target in $\PanelMissQwen$ of its games while still paying $\PanelOwnQwen$ units into a pot
that never reaches $\PanelTarget$; the lottery then takes the remainder in
$\PanelCatQwen$ games and its seats finish with $\PanelPayQwen$. One model buys a
certainty it did not need and the other buys nothing at all, and both end near zero. Only a
benchmark that prices the outcome against what was attainable puts them side by side, which
is why we report the gap rather than the success rate throughout.
