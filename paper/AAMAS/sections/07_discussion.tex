\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Three controls, each removing a different explanation, converge on one description. Moving
the catastrophe risk across its whole range leaves the contribution schedule of
\PanelNFlatWord{} of \PanelNmodelsWord{} models unchanged, and the one model that does
react reacts once, at the point where the risk disappears entirely. Deleting the sentences that supply
the equal split does not reduce contribution in any model and raises two of them to their
endowment cap, so the schedule is not obedience to the prompt. Asking the agents outright
establishes that the arithmetic is available to them and unused. Replacing their opponents
with policies that make the best response computable shows that the schedule does not bend
to the opponent either, not in any of $\EthreeaNgames$ games and not in the
$\EthreeaDominatedGames$ where every alternative is strictly dominated. What these agents
bring to a collective-risk setting is a fixed contribution schedule, close to the focal
split, that is insensitive to the risk, to the prompt's framing and to what the other
agents do.

For a system designer the consequence is asymmetric, and it is the reason we report a gap
to the optimum rather than a success rate. As collaborators these agents are excellent:
they reach the group target in most populations, they do not defect opportunistically, and
they never hold the group hostage. As stakeholders they are unsafe, because they will not
protect the resources entrusted to them --- a scripted free-rider out-earns every model in
the panel (\S\ref{sec:bestresponse}), and a group-level metric records that outcome as a
success. The two failure modes we observe are invisible to such a metric from opposite
sides: \textsc{grok-4.20-non-rea\-son\-ing} reaches the target in
$\PanelReachGrok\%$ of games while finishing with $\PanelPayGrok$ of
$\PanelEndowment$ units, and Qwen pays $\PanelOwnQwen$ units into pots
that never reach the target at all. A deployment that reads only whether the shared goal
was met will rank both as it should not.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Sections~\ref{sec:riskgrid} and~\ref{sec:controls} are self-play: all \PanelNplayersWord{} seats hold one
model. That design is the object of our criticism, and we answer it in
Section~\ref{sec:bestresponse} with fixed opponents rather than with a genuinely mixed
population. Fixed opponents buy exactness --- the best response is enumerable --- at the
cost of realism, since a scripted seat cannot respond to the model as a second model would.
A round-robin over mixed pairs is the natural next measurement and is not reported here.
The panel is \PanelNmodelsWord{} models, one per provider and all at the inexpensive tier, so a
capability effect within a family is outside what we can see; cells hold
$\PanelNpercell$ games, which is why every interval is a bootstrap and every test a
permutation test on games rather than on seats. We run one prompt template at one
temperature, and although the focal-point deletion of Section~\ref{sec:nohint} is a
paraphrase of a kind, it is not a systematic sensitivity analysis. Pricing a gap in
expected payoff assumes risk neutrality; we therefore rest the conclusions on the $p=0$
column and on the strictly dominated profiles, where contributing loses under every
increasing utility and no such assumption is in play. One of the two value probes asks for
a point estimate mid-game and is ambiguous between ``every round'' and ``every remaining
round'' --- several wrong answers are correct readings of the second --- so we report its
$\PanelProbeEvPointAcc\%$ for completeness and rest nothing on it. Finally, the game-level
randomisation is seeded and reproduces, but the model text does not: replaying identical
cells at identical seed and temperature returns different play. We release prompts,
configurations, seeds and code, and promise reproducibility of the design rather than of
the transcripts.

\paragraph{Ethics.}
The study runs no human subjects and collects no personal data. Its result is a negative
one about deployed systems, and we report it in the direction that is useful: agents that
cooperate unconditionally are safe to place in a group and unsafe to place in charge of
something, and the distance between those two sentences is what a measurement against the
best response makes visible.
